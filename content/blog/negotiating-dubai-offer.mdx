Negotiating a job offer in Dubai feels uncomfortable to many candidates. The fear: "What if they withdraw the offer?" The reality: this almost never happens when you negotiate professionally. Employers expect it.

Here's the framework, the numbers, and the exact emails to get 15–25% more than your first offer.

## Why most people don't negotiate — and pay for it

A survey of Gulf professionals found that **only 38% negotiated their last job offer**. Among those who did negotiate, **91% received a higher package** — with a median increase of AED 4,200/month.

Over a 3-year employment period, not negotiating a single offer costs the average Dubai professional AED 150,000+.

## Step 1: Know your number before the conversation

The single biggest mistake candidates make is entering a negotiation without a target number. You need to know:

1. **Your current market rate** — use Addify's Salary Check for your role, city, and experience level
2. **Your walk-away number** — the minimum package you'd accept
3. **Your aspirational number** — what you'd happily accept (typically 15–20% above the initial offer)

## Step 2: Separate base salary from total package

In Dubai, your negotiation levers are:

| Component | Negotiable? | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| Basic salary | Yes | Affects gratuity — employers may resist |
| Housing allowance | Yes | Often more flexible than basic |
| Transport allowance | Sometimes | Smaller but worth asking |
| Annual leave | Sometimes | Try for 25+ days |
| Performance bonus | Yes | Get the structure in writing |
| Sign-on bonus | Yes | One-time, low risk for employer |
| Notice period | Yes | Shorter is better for you |

When you get the initial offer, ask for the full breakdown. If the employer won't provide it, that's a red flag.

## Step 3: Time your ask correctly

The best moment to negotiate is **after you receive a written offer, before you sign**. Verbal offers are not binding — wait for the written offer letter.

Don't negotiate in the interview. Don't negotiate in the same conversation where the offer is made. Take at least 24 hours to review, then respond.

## Step 4: Use the telephone, not email, for the negotiation itself

Negotiations happen faster and with better outcomes on the phone. But you'll need an email to confirm what was agreed. Here's the flow:

1. Respond to the written offer by email acknowledging receipt and requesting a call
2. Have the negotiation call
3. Follow up with a confirming email

## Email templates

### Template 1: Acknowledging the offer and requesting time

> Subject: Re: [Company] Offer — [Your Name]
>
> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> Thank you for the offer letter. I'm genuinely excited about the opportunity at [Company] and the work we discussed.
>
> I'd like to review the details carefully and discuss a few points. Would you be available for a 15-minute call on [Day] or [Day]?
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

### Template 2: Making the counter-offer by email (if call isn't possible)

> Subject: Re: [Company] Offer — Revised Package Discussion
>
> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> Thank you again for the offer. I'm very interested in joining the team and believe this is a strong mutual fit.
>
> Based on my research of market rates for [Role] in Dubai with [X] years of experience, as well as my current package, I'd like to discuss adjusting the offer to [AED X,XXX]/month basic salary with [X]% housing allowance.
>
> [One sentence on why you're worth this — recent achievement, specialized skill, or competing offer.]
>
> I remain enthusiastic about the opportunity and I'm confident we can find a structure that works for both parties. I'm available to discuss further at your convenience.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

### Template 3: Accepting after successful negotiation

> Subject: Offer Acceptance — [Your Name]
>
> Dear [Hiring Manager Name],
>
> Thank you for working with me on the package details. I'm pleased to formally accept the offer for [Role] at [Company] with the revised package of [summarize what was agreed].
>
> I look forward to joining the team on [Start Date]. Please send me any onboarding documentation at your convenience.
>
> Best regards,
> [Your Name]

## What if they say no?

If the employer can't move on salary, try these alternatives:

- **Additional annual leave** — 5 extra days is common and low-cost for employers
- **Work-from-home flexibility** — saves you transport time and cost
- **Earlier performance review** — ask for a 6-month review instead of annual
- **Sign-on bonus** — one-time payment the employer can justify separately from salary bands
- **Professional development budget** — AED 5,000–10,000/year for courses and certifications

## Red flags in the negotiation

Watch out for:
- Employers who say "This is a non-negotiable fixed salary band" and provide no documentation
- Offers that drop significantly if you ask for more time
- Pressure to sign within 24 hours
- Verbal offers with no written follow-up

A good employer will negotiate professionally. An employer who turns aggressive or threatening at negotiation is showing you exactly how they handle disagreement.

## The bottom line

Negotiate. Always. Professionally, confidently, and with data. The worst response you'll get is "We can't move on this." That is useful information about your future employer.

The best response is the one most professionals get: a better package.
